# Directory & listing submissions — free backlinks and visitors

Getting listed where people look for landlord/renting tools does two things: sends visitors directly, and gives you
backlinks that lift all your SEO. Most are free. Do a few a week.

For each, you'll usually need: name (DIY Inventory), URL (https://diyinventory.co.uk), a one-line and a short
description, a logo (public/icons/icon-512.png), and a category (proptech / property / landlord tools).

**Ready descriptions (copy-paste):**
- *One line:* Do your own property inventory, check-in, check-out or inspection on your phone — £29.99, no subscription.
- *Short:* DIY Inventory turns the walk-round into a professional report on any phone: a guided photo checklist for
  every room, the date and time inside each photo, meters, keys, tenant sign-off and a PDF the moment it's signed.
  Free to start, £29.99 to download, no subscription and no VAT. Made to be used as deposit evidence.

## 1. Google Business Profile (do this first)
google.com/business — free, and it can show you in local/Maps results and give a trusted link. Category: "Software
company" or "Property management service". Add the logo, the descriptions above, and the website. Even without a shop
front you can list as a service-area business.

## 2. Microsoft Bing Places
bingplaces.com — the Bing equivalent; quick if you've done Google.

## 3. Software / app directories (free tiers)
- Product Hunt (producthunt.com) — launch it; a good launch sends a spike + a lasting link.
- AlternativeTo (alternativeto.net) — list as an alternative to "inventory clerk" / inventory software. High-intent.
- SaaSHub, Capterra, GetApp, G2 (free vendor listing) — property/real-estate software categories.
- Crunchbase — a basic company profile (link + description).

## 4. UK landlord & proptech resource lists
Search Google for these and submit / email the owner to be added:
- "best apps for landlords" / "landlord tools UK" roundups (many accept suggestions).
- "property inventory software" / "inventory app" listicles.
- Landlord association resource pages (NRLA-style member resource lists), local landlord forums' "useful links".
- Proptech directories (e.g. "UK proptech companies" lists).
Pitch email: "I run DIY Inventory, a phone app that lets landlords and tenants do their own dated-photo inventory for
£29.99. I noticed your [list/page] — would you consider adding it? Here's a one-liner: [paste]. Happy to return the
favour." Keep it short and personal.

## 5. Free-tool aggregators
Sites that list free tools will take your **free property report** and **free inventory template** and the new
**inventory cost calculator** (/inventory-cost-calculator) — pitch the free thing, not the paid app; the visitor lands
and discovers the rest.

## 6. Review sites (let it build naturally)
Trustpilot — set up a free profile and invite real customers (e.g. in the finished-report email) to review. Never
write or buy fake reviews. A handful of genuine reviews adds trust and a link.

## Tracking
After each listing, check your analytics → referrers over the next weeks to see which actually send visitors, and
spend time on those. Many listings send little traffic directly but the backlink still helps Google — that's fine.

## Don't
- Don't pay for bulk "500 directory submissions" — they're spam links that can hurt.
- Don't write fake reviews or fabricate stats.
- Keep every description honest: £29.99, no VAT, no subscription, "made to be used as evidence", clerk prices "typical".
