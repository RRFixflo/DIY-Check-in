For a letting agency, inventories are one of those costs that grow quietly with every tenancy. A check-in and a check-out for each move, often a mid-term inspection too, and each one either a clerk’s fee or a member of staff out of the office for an hour or two. This guide looks at where the cost comes from and the options for bringing it down without weakening the record you rely on at the end of the tenancy.

Where the cost of inventories comes from

  • The visit itself: a clerk typically charges around £100 to £250 a report depending on area and property size, more for large or furnished homes.
  • Staff time: if your own staff do them, it’s travel, the walk-round and writing up, usually two hours or more per property.
  • Booking and rebooking: matching a clerk’s diary to move-in day, and re-arranging when keys or tenants are late.
  • Chasing paperwork: getting reports signed, filed and found again when the deposit is returned.

Multiply that by every move in and out across a year and it’s often thousands of pounds a branch.

Three ways to cut it

1. Negotiate volume with your clerk

If you send a clerk steady work, ask for a volume rate. It helps, but you still pay for a visit every time, and you’re still working around their diary.

2. Do them in-house

Inventory software on a staff tablet removes the clerk’s fee but not the staff time. It suits agencies with a dedicated property manager who is out anyway.

3. Let the tenant do it, properly

Tenant self check-in removes the visit completely. The risk is a thin record: a few photos in an email won’t help anyone at the end of the tenancy. What matters is that the tenant is guided through every room and item, that the date and time are part of each photo, that meters and keys are recorded, and that both sides can sign. That’s what a structured self check-in gives you.

Questions

Is a tenant self check-in as good as a clerk’s report?

A clerk brings independence and experience. A structured self check-in brings speed and cost, and if every item is photographed with the date, meters and keys recorded and both sides sign, it is a full record. Many agencies use self check-in for standard flats and a clerk for large or high-value furnished homes.

How do we stop tenants leaving things out?

Use an app that guides them item by item and won’t let a room be finished without a photo of every item. The other party can then add comments before signing.

Who pays for the inventory?

Under the Tenant Fees Act 2019 tenants in England can’t be charged for an inventory, so the cost sits with the landlord or the agent. That’s one reason agencies look for a cheaper way to do them.

Can we use it for mid-term inspections too?

Yes. Send the tenant the same link and they choose an inspection instead of a check-in; it can start from the check-in report so changes are easy to spot.
