This is general information to help you record a property accurately. It isn’t legal advice, and the rules on deposits differ between countries and tenancies. For a dispute, speak to a qualified professional.

Everything in a home wears with normal use. In many places a tenant isn’t expected to return a property in the condition it started in, only in the condition you’d expect after normal use for that length of time. A good inventory makes the difference between wear and damage easy to see.

Usually wear

  • Carpet slightly flattened along the main walkways.
  • Light scuffs on walls behind doors and furniture.
  • Faded curtains or paint where the sun falls.
  • Loose door handles or tired hinges after years of use.
  • Minor scratches on well-used worktops.

Usually damage

  • Burns, large stains or tears in carpets.
  • Holes in walls, or unapproved redecoration.
  • Broken furniture, glass or appliances.
  • Damage from leaks or neglect that wasn’t reported.
  • Missing items listed at the check-in.

What decides it

  • The starting condition: without a check-in record, it’s hard to show anything changed.
  • How long the tenancy was, and how many people lived there.
  • The age and quality of the item at the start.

How an inventory helps

Record every item’s condition at the check-in, with photos that carry the date and time. At the check-out, compare each item with its check-in record. DIY Inventory does this side by side, so normal wear and real damage are easy to tell apart.
